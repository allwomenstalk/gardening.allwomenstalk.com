# 7 Garden Plants You Can Easily Grow in a Pot at Home

The pleasure of planting and growing your own food is truly unique. More than ever, people are looking for rewarding ways to cultivate fresh produce right at home. Even if you do not have a spacious backyard or dedicated garden plot, container gardening makes it entirely possible to harvest delicious ingredients on a balcony, patio, or sunny windowsill. Here is a practical list of garden plants that thrive in pots and containers.

| Plant Type | Recommended Pot Size | Sunlight Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh Herbs | 6 to 8 inches | 6+ hours |
| Tomatoes | 5+ gallons | 6 to 8 hours |
| Leafy Greens | 8 to 10 inches | 4 to 6 hours |

## 1. Number of Herbs

Herbs are amongst the most basic garden plants I can easily grow in a pot. You too can choose from a variety of herbs like basil, rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage, chives, and parsley. If you do not have too much space, you can even grow them all together in one large-sized pot to create a convenient kitchen herb garden. For step-by-step planting tips, check out the [Royal Horticultural Society guide to herbs](https://www.rhs.org.uk/herbs/growing).

- **Basil & Parsley:** Thrives in warm, sunny spots with consistent moisture.
- **Rosemary & Thyme:** Drought-tolerant perennials that prefer well-draining soil.
- **Chives & Sage:** Low-maintenance options perfect for beginner container gardeners.

## 2. Citrus Fruits

If you happen to live in a climate with seasonal shifts, dwarf citrus fruits are a wonderful option for plants to grow in a pot. You can easily grow dwarf lemons, oranges, and limes as long as you ensure that the plants get ample direct sunlight during the day and are brought indoors during frost. For care instructions, review the [University of Minnesota Extension citrus guide](https://extension.umn.edu/container-gardening/growing-citrus-containers).

## 3. Tomatoes

Tomato seeds are typically grown in small starter pots before being transplanted into larger containers or garden beds. Instead of moving them outdoors to a full garden plot, they grow remarkably well when kept in deep containers. You will need to insert a sturdy stake or wire cage into the pot to support heavy stems as fruit develops. Pair your setup with [leafy green vegetables](#7) for a complete home salad garden. Learn more from the [Farmers' Almanac tomato care tips](https://www.almanac.com/plant/tomatoes).

## 4. Mushrooms

Mushrooms are also fantastic garden plants you can cultivate in pots or shaded indoor containers. Just make sure that you choose certified edible varieties such as oyster or button mushrooms from reliable spawn kits. You can plant different compatible varieties in container trays or pots if you have limited space in your home.

## 5. Melons

While standard large watermelons require extensive garden space, compact mini varieties thrive in containers. Golden Midget melons, Jenny Lind melons, and small cantaloupes are ideal choices for container culture. Ensure your container is deep enough and provide a small trellis for vertical climbing.

## 6. Squash

Squash varieties are also great garden plants to grow in containers. Because they expand quickly, you will need slightly larger containers for these robust plants; a 5-gallon bucket or pot with drainage holes is quite sufficient. You can cultivate zucchini, summer squash, bush acorns, and compact pumpkins indoors or on a patio. Discover more helpful ideas for [home gardening projects](https://gardening.allwomenstalk.com/) on our site.

## 7. Leafy Green Vegetables

There are numerous leafy greens that grow swiftly and effortlessly in home containers. Collard greens, kale, Swiss chard, lettuce, and spinach yield continuous harvests in shallow pots. They require plenty of sunlight and do best placed near south-facing windows or on sunny balconies. You can complement your greens by growing [fresh kitchen herbs](#1) in neighboring containers.

These are just a few of the garden plants that are easy to grow in pots. To ensure success, maintain proper soil pH balance, adequate water drainage, and sufficient air circulation so water does not stagnate and harm root systems.
