# A Beginner\-Friendly Way to Start Attracting Hummingbirds to Your Garden

Hummingbirds are some of the most entertaining and delightful visitors you can welcome to your backyard. If you've been wondering how to attract hummingbirds to your garden, you are in the right place. While there are many ways to attract hummingbirds, combining a few simple techniques is the most effective approach. The following list includes some traditional methods to draw in more of these tiny fliers to your yard, along with a couple of ideas you might not have considered before. Integrating these methods into your routine can make drawing them in much easier, especially when you start by [hanging hummingbird feeders](#5) and [planting nectar-rich flowers](#1).

| Method | Primary Benefit | Difficulty Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Multiple Feeders | Prevents territory bullying | Easy |
| Red Ribbons | Attracts birds from a distance | Very Easy |
| Native Flowers | Provides natural, long-term food | Medium |

## 5. Hang Hummingbird Feeders

This is probably the most common way people think of to attract hummingbirds. The best time to hang feeders is in May, but sometimes they arrive earlier in the month of April. If you're not sure which type to buy, check out this guide on [choosing the right hummingbird feeder](https://allwomenstalk.com/humming-bird-feeders/). You might want to wait until you spot your first little visitor before you start hanging feeders full of nectar, or hang one early to welcome the first migrants. You can find high-quality, durable options from reputable brands like [Perky-Pet](https://www.perkypet.com/) to get started. Also, to answer a common question: are hummingbirds afraid of other birds? While they are small, they are surprisingly brave and will defend their feeding area, but they are often most defensive against other hummingbirds.

## 4. Offer Multiple Feeders to Prevent Bullying

Many male hummingbirds tend to be highly territorial, which often leads to fighting. They have been known to bully one another while trying to get food. One dominant bird might act as a hummingbird bully, scaring away the others from the food source. If you are wondering what to do about bully hummingbirds, the solution is simple. Hanging multiple feeders in a number of spread-out locations out of sight of each other will give everyone a chance to feed at their own station. Understanding how to stop a bully hummingbird by separating the feeders ensures a much more peaceful backyard habitat.

## 3. Dangle Red Ribbons from Fences or Trees

The color red draws hummingbirds in from afar due to their highly developed color vision. If you are using [feeders with clear nectar](#5) and no bright colors on the feeder itself, try hanging red ribbons from the perches. You can also tie a red ribbon around the tree trunk or branch where the feeder is hanging. This vibrant color will let hummingbirds know there’s something tasty waiting for them, making it a simple yet effective way of attracting hummingbirds to your yard.

## 2. Set up a Mist System for Them

Hummingbirds like baths, just like most other birds, but they prefer mist over deep water. These little super-fliers have been seen darting in and out of sprinklers as lawns are being watered, preening their feathers on the fly. They will come right to a misting system for their daily bath. You can set up a simple garden mister, like those from [Orbit](https://www.orbitonline.com/), to create a refreshing mist throughout the day. Since these birds are already visiting your garden for [nectar-rich flowers](#1), adding a mist system will make your yard their favorite hangout.

## 1. Plant Flowers They Are Drawn to

Planting a variety of nectar-producing plants is the most natural way to support these birds. Flowers hummingbirds are attracted to include many vibrant varieties. You might wonder, do bougainvillea attract hummingbirds? Yes, these climbing vines are a fantastic choice alongside other favorites. And if you're wondering, do hummingbirds like sunflowers? While they prefer tube-shaped blossoms, sunflowers can still provide perching spots and attract the tiny insects that make up a crucial part of their diet. To find native varieties that thrive in your specific region, consult resources like the [National Audubon Society](https://www.audubon.org/) database. For more plant ideas, check out this list of [flowers that naturally attract hummingbirds](https://gardening.allwomenstalk.com/5-flowers-that-will-attract-hummingbirds/).

Consider planting some of these top-rated favorites:

- Honeysuckle and morning glory
- Butterfly bush and bee balm
- Petunias, impatiens, and foxglove
- Mimosa and four o'clocks

You will be able to bring in many hummingbirds by combining two or three of these methods. If you're feeling handy, you might also enjoy [building your own bird feeder](https://gardening.allwomenstalk.com/5-tips-on-making-your-own-bird-feeder/) to add to your yard. What ways have you used to attract hummingbirds that seemed to work best of all? We would love to hear your gardening success stories!

Here is a quick checklist for hummingbird success:

- Keep feeders clean and filled with fresh nectar.
- Position multiple feeders out of sight of each other.
- Plant a variety of red or bright tubular flowers.
